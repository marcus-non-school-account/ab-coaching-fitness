The honest diagnosis

The ceiling you have hit
is not a technical one.

You already train as much as your body allows. You drill, you compete, you watch footage. The people beating you now are not out-teching you.

You get out-muscled, not out-skilled

Someone with your exact game but more force behind it takes the grip, settles into the position and rides out the clock. No amount of extra drilling closes a strength gap.

The last round is where it goes

You are sharp for four minutes and surviving by six. Everything you know is still in your head. Your body has just stopped being able to execute it at speed.

You are training around damage

Shoulders, knees, lower back. Nothing bad enough to stop you — just enough to quietly cap how hard you are willing to go, every session, for years.

The gym is not extra jiu-jitsu. It is the part that lets you use the jiu-jitsu you already have — for a full match, against people your own size, without paying for it all week.

Who this is for

Good, and going after
something bigger.

Augusto works with competitors, not beginners. What changes between these three is how much of your year is built around a date on the calendar.

Winning locally

You clean up at home and stall at the bigger ones

Your game works against the people you train with every week. Step up a level and it stops landing, because the athlete across from you is simply harder to move. This is where a first proper strength block changes the most.

On the podium

Medalling, and chasing the top step

You are close enough that the gap is measurable. Training gets periodised backwards from your competition date — heavy early, sharp late, with the conditioning work that decides matches once both people are tired.

Professional

Competing for a living, and intending to keep doing it

A full calendar, real money on the line, and a body that has to survive all of it. The work here is availability — managing load so you arrive at every one of them able to perform.

How it works

The first month

One of the most important parts of coaching happens before the programme even begins.

Step 01

We map your week first

How many times you are on the mats, when you compete next, what is currently sore, and how many hours you genuinely have for the gym. Nothing gets written before that conversation.

Step 02

The plan fits around training

Sessions are placed so they never blunt your hardest mat days. Every lift is demonstrated. You are not guessing at what to do or how heavy to go.

Step 03

We adjust it weekly

Weekly check-ins, form corrected from video, and loads tracked so progress is a number rather than a feeling. If something has not moved in a month, we both know.
