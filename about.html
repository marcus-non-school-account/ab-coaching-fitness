The long way round

Work was never
the hard part

My parents split up when I was seven, so my sister and I were raised mostly by our mother. I was the only man in the house, so I felt I had to hustle. I started working at thirteen in my granny’s printing company and never went to college. My dad is a real work horse. He is where I learned about diligence.

At sixteen my cousin brought me to a gym for the first time. I never stopped lifting after that. I wanted to be a footballer, but I was not good enough for it — sorry, Mom.

Dublin, 2017

Fifty hour weeks,
and still on the mats

When I moved to Dublin I worked jobs that ranged from washing dishes to cleaning tables to serving people fresh fish. Ten euro an hour. I started training jiu-jitsu in 2019 and I have had over fifty competitive matches since.

I was working fifty-plus hours a week and still finding time to lift and grapple most days. My colleagues would ask me how. The honest answer was that I wanted the reps. I wanted to sharpen the skill.

Two years later I was a qualified personal trainer. I hired mentors, worked in two gyms, and kept grappling as much as my body would allow. I had found the thing I cared about and turned it into the job.

How I coach

Experience is
the best teacher

My family taught me to work hard, be diligent and respect other people. That is more or less the whole method.

You should understand the why

Every athlete gets the reasoning alongside the programme. Why this lift, why this week, why we are backing off before a comp. You should be able to coach yourself by the end of it.

Nobody trains alone

You get access to a group of people chasing the same thing — posting numbers, admitting the bad weeks, keeping each other honest. It is the part most people underestimate.

Progress gets written down

Loads and targets tracked every session so neither of us is guessing. If a lift has not moved in a month, we both know about it, and we change something.

Off the mats

Take the recovery
as seriously as the work

I spent nine days in Spain this August. A few swims, some lifting, plenty of time in the sun. It was exactly what I needed. I have been focusing more on myself this year — reflecting, spending proper time with friends and family.

You never know what the future holds. Life can change completely in twelve months, so I try to be present and enjoy the things that matter. I coach the same way: hard work, then real recovery. Athletes who skip the second half do not last.
